# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: September 15, 2026*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Home by Five gives multi-location service businesses one trusted view of marketing performance and a clear answer about what to do next.
**What it does:** Home by Five connects marketing spend, demand, leads, bookings, revenue, market opportunity, local visibility, conversion, and capacity across locations. It separates market problems from marketing, operational, attribution, and capacity problems, then turns the evidence into prioritized decisions. Higher plans add strategy and direct human guidance.
**Product category:** Marketing decision intelligence for multi-location service businesses.
**Product type:** Subscription software with optional strategic and human service layers.
**Business model:** Three founding monthly subscriptions: Numbers & Decisions at $100, Numbers, Decisions & Strategy at $500, and Numbers, Decisions, Strategy & Human Touch at $1,000.

## Target Audience
**Target companies:** Franchise systems and multi-location service providers in any service category. Phase 1 should prioritize franchisors and franchise owners responsible for network economics, marketing fund performance, and location growth.
**Decision-makers:** Founders, franchise brand owners, CEOs, presidents of operations, CMOs, heads of growth, and finance leaders responsible for marketing allocation.
**Primary use case:** Decide where the next marketing dollar and the next hour of attention should go across locations and channels.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Replace fragmented reports with one operating picture tied to revenue.
- Find which locations and channels need investment, diagnosis, protection, or better data.
- Turn recurring performance questions into a ranked action list.
- Give leadership enough clarity to make the decision and finish the workday.
**Use cases:**
- Compare markets fairly after accounting for different opportunity sizes.
- Separate weak demand from weak visibility, conversion, operations, or capacity.
- Reconcile channel performance when vendors and finance show different numbers.
- Find local execution gaps that prevent national brand strength from compounding.
- Allocate incremental budget using opportunity and unit economics rather than equal splits or politics.

## Problems and Pain Points
**Core problem:** Multi-location leaders have more marketing data than answers. Performance is fragmented across locations, channels, vendors, websites, CRMs, call tracking, and finance systems, so leadership cannot confidently decide what to change.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Dashboards describe activity but do not separate market, marketing, conversion, operator, data, and capacity constraints.
- Channel vendors grade their own work and can produce conflicting versions of success.
- Equal-budget or historical allocation ignores marginal opportunity and unit economics.
- Total corporate control blocks local relevance, while total local freedom prevents consistency and learning.
**What it costs them:** Wasted spend, missed demand, weak locations hidden by network averages, excessive reporting time, and slower decisions.
**Emotional tension:** Leaders suspect money is leaking but cannot prove where, and every answer creates another meeting, spreadsheet, or vendor debate.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Multi-location marketing analytics and intelligence products — often stop at reporting, reputation, listings, or channel visibility rather than one economic decision layer.
**Secondary:** Agencies, consultants, BI dashboards, attribution tools, franchise management platforms, and individual channel vendors — each can solve part of the problem but usually owns only one layer or has an incentive to defend its own output.
**Indirect:** Spreadsheets, monthly vendor decks, equal budget splits, intuition, and executive meetings — familiar but difficult to audit or scale.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Decisions, not a checklist of SEO, PPC, social, or reporting deliverables.
- Location, channel, market, conversion, attribution, and capacity context in one view.
- Transparent rules, ranges, caveats, and evidence rather than unexplained scores.
- Centralized intelligence with corporate standards and controlled local freedom.
- A service ladder that lets the buyer choose software clarity, strategy, or a human in the loop.
**How we do it differently:** Home by Five starts with the economic question, shows the evidence behind the answer, and makes uncertainty visible. It is designed to distinguish what kind of problem exists before recommending more spend.
**Why that is better:** Leadership can prioritize fewer actions, explain the decision to stakeholders, and avoid paying for demand a location cannot convert or fulfill.
**Why customers choose us:** They want a trusted answer to “what should we do next?” without adding another disconnected report.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| We already have dashboards and agencies. | Home by Five is the decision layer across them. It reconciles the economic picture and shows which question still lacks evidence. |
| Our locations are too different to compare. | The model makes market potential, local visibility, conversion, capacity, and source quality explicit instead of comparing raw revenue alone. |
| We do not trust attribution. | Unresolved revenue stays visible, channel causation is not assumed, and channel ROI is withheld when source coverage is insufficient. |
| This sounds like more work for franchisees. | The system is designed for leadership control and clear local actions, not another reporting burden for operators. |
| The prices seem too low or the scope seems broad. | These are founding subscription prices. Data access, integrations, location limits, and service scope must be confirmed during onboarding and validated through buyer calls. |

**Anti-persona:** Single-location companies with no recurring cross-location allocation problem; teams seeking guaranteed revenue claims; organizations unwilling to share or improve the minimum data needed for a defensible answer.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Conflicting reports, marketing-fee distrust, slow executive decisions, weak-location surprises, and pressure to justify the next budget increase.
**Pull:** One view, a ranked action list, explainable findings, and a service level that can include strategy or a human partner.
**Habit:** Existing vendor relationships, spreadsheet routines, equal budget allocation, and the belief that local differences make comparison impossible.
**Anxiety:** Integration effort, data quality, corporate versus local control, another tool nobody uses, and whether recommendations will be credible.

## Customer Language
**How they describe the problem:**
- “Where should the next marketing dollar go?”
- “Why does a strong national brand lose locally?”
- “Where does demand leak before revenue?”
- “Why do we have multiple versions of the truth?”
- “Is this a weak market, weak marketing, a weak operator, weak conversion, or a capacity constraint?”
**How they describe us:**
- “One trusted view of what is happening and what to do next.”
- “The decision layer across our locations and channels.”
**Words to use:** numbers, decisions, next move, booked work, location health, market opportunity, visibility, source coverage, capacity, guardrails, evidence, clear priorities.
**Words to avoid:** magic, guaranteed, autonomous growth, perfect attribution, brand equity loss when only search undercapture is measured, false precision, unexplained AI claims.
**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Potential | A normalized index of how attractive a local market appears based on demand, serviceable market, and competitive headroom |
| Presence | A normalized index of how visible the brand is locally; it is not market share |
| Local Demand Capture | Estimated share of addressable commercial demand captured by a location |
| Source coverage | Share of revenue with a usable marketing-source match |
| Monthly opportunity | A scenario range for additional monthly revenue using demand, conversion, job value, and capacity assumptions |
| Decision queue | Ranked actions with their evidence, confidence, and next step |
