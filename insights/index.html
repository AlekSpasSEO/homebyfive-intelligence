Why a strong national brand can still lose local demand

The working research question is not whether fragmented local execution matters. It is how much commercial opportunity is undercaptured, where the gap appears, and which governance and execution signals are associated with stronger local results.

The study begins with a nine-market pilot across restoration, commercial cleaning, and HVAC. It will only scale after market boundaries, intent definitions, data collection, score rules, and revenue translation can be applied consistently.

Methodology

What the research must prove.

The uncomfortable number is the conclusion, not the premise.

1. Define the addressable local demand

Build a deduplicated commercial-intent demand universe for a defined service territory. Keep informational research and separate B2B demand universes out of the consumer opportunity denominator.

2. Estimate local demand capture

Model the click or visit opportunity from observed search and Maps presence. Keep organic, local, paid, community, and AI-recommendation signals separate until their behavior is understood.

3. Compare inside the same brand first

Top-versus-bottom location comparisons hold national brand strength relatively constant. Market-size differences and territory boundaries still need explicit control.

4. Translate gaps conservatively

Move from missed demand to visits, leads, booked work, and revenue using ranges. Label each input as observed, benchmarked, or modeled. Add profit only when defensible margin data exists.

5. Preserve what the evidence cannot say

Search visibility is not total brand equity. Correlation is not causation. Manual scoring needs a rubric. Every source, date, geography, extraction method, and assumption must be auditable.
